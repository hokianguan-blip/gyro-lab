Rigidity grows with: ① faster spin, ② heavier rotor, ③ weight concentrated at the rim.

Why it matters on the aircraft

POSITION GYROSGyro horizon (attitude indicator) and directional gyro use rigidity in space. The rotor holds its position while the aircraft moves around it — that is how the instrument shows pitch, bank and heading.

Slow rotor = weak rigidity = unreliable reading. That is why a run-down gyro wanders and why the instrument must reach full speed before flight.

Precession

Push a spinning wheel and it does not tip where you pushed. It reacts 90° later, in the direction of spin. Push at 12 → feel it at 3 (for a clockwise wheel).

Why it matters on the aircraft

RATE GYROSTurn and slip indicator and turn coordinator use precession. When the aircraft turns, it pushes on the gyro; the gyro responds 90° away, tilting its gimbal against a spring. The tilt is a measure of the turn rate.

Same rule on a propeller: a clockwise prop (pilot's view) pitched nose-up tends to yaw the nose to the right.
